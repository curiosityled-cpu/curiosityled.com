# Launch kit (internal)

The delivery runbook trimmed to what an implementer needs in the room. The full
version, with acceptance wording and the hours log, is
[`docs/delivery-runbook.md`](../delivery-runbook.md).

## The six phases, with their checkpoint

| Phase | Time | Checkpoint — not done until |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0. Before kickoff | 0.5 h | Tenant record exists with a package and a named owner |
| 1. Kickoff | 1 h | Outcome, manager population, cadence, report layout and data handling agreed |
| 2. Access and roster | 1.5 h | One manager has completed a real check-in in the live tenant |
| 3. Configuration | 1.5 h | Customer owner has seen the report layout with real week-one data |
| 4. Training | 0.5 h | A manager other than the owner has opened the app |
| 5. Launch | 0.5 h | The customer can point at a check-in that arrived |
| 6. 30-day review | 0.5 h | One change for month two agreed in writing |

Five hours of implementation per tenant. Anything beyond that is logged with a
written cause, in the runbook's hours log.

## The five things the customer receives

1. `setup-checklist.md` — what to send us.
2. `manager-quick-start.md` — the one-pager for managers.
3. `report-template.md` — the month-one layout, with a fictional worked example.
4. `visibility-and-approval-guide.md` — who sees what, and who approves a change.
5. This launch kit.

## Three rules that keep a launch from slipping

- No phase starts before the previous checkpoint is met; the launch date moves
  instead.
- Package boundaries are confirmed at phase 0 (reviews and 360 need Growth or
  above; the talent bench needs Organization), never discovered in phase 3.
- Nothing on the "Not yet published" list appears in a proposal: no launch
  assurance, no AI allowance, no multi-unit promise, no connector beyond the four
  live ones.
